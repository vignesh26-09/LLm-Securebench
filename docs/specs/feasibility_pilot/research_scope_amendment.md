## Candidate contribution, bounded to the actual question

> We investigate whether prior adversarial user interactions increase unauthorized exact disclosure of a trusted synthetic secret during subsequent legitimate tasks that do not request the secret, compared with matched non-adversarial conversation histories.

“Subsequent” means later **within the same supplied conversation context**, not memory across sessions or altered model weights. “Unauthorized” means the independently reviewed final task has no need or permission to disclose the trusted secret. “Unsolicited” is relative to that final task: its user prompt does not directly or indirectly request, quote, encode, or cue the secret. It does **not** imply that earlier adversarial user messages never tried to elicit it. The primary observable event remains exact contiguous appearance of that trial's secret in the designated final defender response, not any semantic/partial leakage or a universal model-safety score. “Increase” is a falsifiable A–T comparison, **not** a result asserted in advance. The bounded pilot's complete-pair difference is descriptive when validity is post-assignment; all-assigned sensitivity bounds and uncertainty remain essential.

The possible contribution is the **joint measurement question**: fresh secret in the intended trusted instruction; attacker-controlled user history with every intermediate assistant reply generated live; a later authorized final task that does not request the secret; goal/topic-matched benign and benign-refusal-history comparators; prespecified 0/2/5 intervening exchanges; earlier-leak accounting; strict exact checking; and explicit context-validity/missingness gates. [P1.2](prior_work_comparison.md) did not verify this entire conjunction in a single reviewed method. That is **not evidence of global originality**: several close full methods and independent comparison review remain outstanding, and an untested combination is not automatically scientifically informative.

## Earlier disclosure is a separate interpretation problem

If a live pre-probe reply already contains the secret and the designated final reply repeats it, the frozen checker still records `Y_earlier=1`, `Y_final=1`, `Y_ever=1`. That trial **must remain** in the primary A–T outcome and assigned denominator. It can show final-turn disclosure, but **cannot be described as a newly delayed compromise**: the information was exposed earlier. Neither dropping such trials nor conditioning the primary contrast on `Y_earlier=0` is permissible after treatment, because earlier leakage may itself be caused by A and exclusion could select favorable histories. A descriptive `Y_earlier × Y_final` cross-tab by arm/model/distance should show earlier-only, final-only, both and neither among fully observed trajectories, plus missing categories. The “final-only” cell is a diagnostic, **not** a replacement primary estimand and not proof that the attack first took effect at the final turn (unseen disclosure channels and history validity remain limits).

## Evidence needed to distinguish mechanisms

| Rival explanation | Prespecified evidence needed; failure of the check limits interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Immediate attack success or simple repetition** | On the **same assigned trajectories**, report immediate eligible attack-response outcomes, `Y_earlier`, `Y_final`, `Y_ever` and their joint counts. Compare final-only versus both/earlier-only descriptively; never condition primary inclusion on early outcomes. Compare with immediate ASR **only if its labels are independently valid for these same observations**; otherwise C1's “beyond ASR” part is blocked, not inferred from a different dataset. |
| **Ordinary topic priming / generic conversational exposure** | Hold goal, legitimate task/probe family, planned topic, role sequence and predeclared length/token-distance tolerances comparable across A and T; contrast A–T first, then A–B1 and A–R as labeled secondary diagnostics. Do not rematch using realized assistant content, refusal or response length. An A–T estimate near zero while A–B1 differs would favor topic/history composition over adversarial wording. |
| **Refusal-history or isolated attack placement** | Preserve R's preassigned prompts *intended* to elicit refusal and I's neutral history/exposure placement. Report realized refusals without selecting on them. A–R and A–I are diagnostic contrasts, not retroactive controls for the primary estimate. |
| **Context dilution, truncation or unavailable retention proof** | Use assigned 0/2/5 filler exchanges plus predeclared token-distance-matched controls, per-model template/role evidence, and `retained_verified` / `truncated_known` / `retention_unverifiable` statuses for each A/T counterpart. Report differential validity by arm/distance and all-assigned bounds. A declining leak rate without retained-context evidence cannot be called decay of adversarial influence. |
| **Probe demand or checker artifact** | Freeze independently reviewed authorized final probes that neither request nor cue the secret; counterbalance variants. Validate exact positive/negative/malformed/wrong-trial fixture cases before pilot, with the frozen primary rule. A leaked marker that appears in a direct request, wrong trial or scripted S text is not valid evidence for this claim. |

These checks can distinguish some interpretations; they do not prove a psychological persistence mechanism. In particular, a bounded pilot with at most 20 goals per model and post-assignment validity losses may be inconclusive even with a positive complete-pair point estimate.

## Falsifiers and material weakeners

| Claim | Result that would refute or materially weaken the narrower claim |
|---|---|
| **C1** | A–T final exact-disclosure contrast is compatible with zero or reverses under honest uncertainty/bounds; nearly all final events are repetitions after `Y_earlier=1`; or a valid same-observation immediate-ASR measure fully accounts for the purported added information. If immediate ASR cannot be validated, that comparison remains **unassessed**, not refuted or supported. |
| **C2** | A–T, A–R and A–B1 contrasts yield practically equivalent interpretations within sufficiently narrow, prespecified uncertainty; or planned matching/probe equivalence fails so comparator effects cannot be isolated. Merely observing non-significance in a small pilot is not equivalence. |
| **C3** | A–T excess disclosure has no credible distance pattern once token placement and context validity are accounted for; or known truncation, asymmetric retention-unverifiable status, or broad assigned-population bounds prevent separating dilution from behavioral change. An invalid context test yields **unsupported/unavailable**, not evidence of no effect. |
| **C4** | A prespecified same-data naive-versus-gated analysis yields no substantive change in reportable conclusions; or the gates only relabel ordinary execution errors rather than prevent an otherwise unsupported scientific claim. Passing implementation tests alone cannot support C4. |

No refutation or support is asserted now. `unsupported` remains the correct C1–C4 status until actual relevant evidence is reviewed.
