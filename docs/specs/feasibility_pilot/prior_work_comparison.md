# Phase 1 method-level comparison (P1.2)

Reviewed 2026-10-14 against the [approved design](design.md) and [source ledger](prior_work_sources.md). The comparison target is **the combined scientific construct**, not a metric name: an attacker-controlled user history in a live conversation, a secret allocated independently to trusted instructions, a later **legitimate** task that does not request the secret, and exact disclosure at that final response, contrasted with matched benign/refusal histories and prespecified context distance. The A–T contrast is a bounded, paired descriptive risk difference under the design's eligibility/missingness rules; it is not an unrestricted all-assigned causal estimate.

`P` = present in the inspected evaluated method; `A` = absent from the explicitly described evaluated method, not a claim about all possible extensions; `NV` = not verified from a sufficient method source. For mixed constructs, notes state what overlaps and what does not. In particular, a benign sample used to validate a judge is **not** a matched experimental control. A deterministic substring check for a marker may be `P` even if the study's primary outcome is broader. ## Cell traces and construct distinctions

- **JBB** — [§§3.1–3.6](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/63092d79154adebd7305dfd498cbff70-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf) define prompt/response harmful-goal success and an LLM judge. The benign examples are for **judge calibration** (§3.5), not topic-matched histories. Its evaluated response unit is a jailbreak response, not a later legitimate secret-disclosure probe. The eight `A` entries refer to this evaluated benchmark protocol; alternative user extensions are outside this coding.
- **HARM, MULTI, SEC** — publisher records establish standardized red-teaming, diverse multi-turn harmful-intent attacks, and six sequence/refusal-timing assessments respectively. Their full evaluated protocols were not traced in this pass. All eight cells remain `NV`, particularly for the SEC final journal version, which must not be inferred from its earlier arXiv title.
- **DOJO** — the [official abstract](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/97091a5177d8dc64b1da8bf3e1f6fb54-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) explicitly describes dynamic agent interaction over legitimate tasks and untrusted tool outputs. That supports live agent replies, but cannot settle the seven more specific conditions. Its attacker controls a **tool-data** surface, not the proposed user-history surface.
- **INJ** — [official record](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.624/) verifies indirect tool-result injection and private-data exfiltration. This is a substantive security-outcome neighbor, but the abstract cannot decide the eight detailed protocol cells.
- **SR** — [official abstract](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e2e06adf560b0706d3b1ddfca9f29756-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) explicitly uses an automated usefulness/harmfulness evaluator with human agreement, not a designated secret's exact-disclosure predicate. Its other method cells require full-paper trace.
- **CRES** — [official USENIX description](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/russinovich) explicitly has successive attacker turns referring to defender-generated responses. The final target is a harmful response; without a full method audit, the exact control and timing details remain `NV`.
- **LEAK** — [full PDF §§3–4, 6](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.94.pdf) has live turn-1 response followed by a turn-2 challenger, and reports leakage at both turns (`P*` = marginal turn-level reporting; a per-trajectory earlier-by-final cross-tab was not verified). Both user turns **ask for protected prompt contents**; the second is not a benign final task. Its ablations change the attack wording/timing, not T and R histories; the tested distance is fixed at two turns, and leakage is detected with ROUGE-L rather than a single fresh exact secret. Defense ASR differences are not the proposed paired A–T effect. This directly overlaps multi-turn system-prompt leakage but differs from delayed unsolicited disclosure under a neutral final probe.
- **PRIV** — [full §§3.2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30094v2) uses a user-supplied secret and topic-conditioned, generated/refined Q/A pairs. The target model receives this **scripted history** and a direct secret-retrieval probe; it did not generate the intervening assistant replies in the evaluated dialogue. It manipulates drift lengths 0/2/3/4/5/6 (`P`) and reports a deterministic normalized-substring result alongside a hybrid judge (`P`, though not the design's strict contiguous exact marker). No A/T/R attack-history contrast or earlier live leak opportunity appears in the evaluated construction. Chi-square association across drift is not a paired adversarial-minus-benign estimand. This is direct overlap for delayed secret redisclosure and distance, with a materially different question and trust boundary.
- **MED** — [full Methods/Results](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2842987) and [Supplement 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12717619/) specify live patient dialogues and same-scenario injection/control executions. In the main six-turn protocol the injection is placed in the turn-3 user treatment request and the primary response is turn 4; the turn-6 outcome follows another user safety inquiry. In the distinct client-side proof of concept, injection occurs at turn 1 and later user requests continue through turn 4. Thus `P*` for delayed legitimate probing applies particularly to the proof of concept, **not** as a claim that the main turn-4 response follows intervening benign exchanges. Paired outcome rates and McNemar comparisons qualify as `P` for a comparable contrast. `P*` earlier-event accounting means the proof of concept tracks first *therapeutic mention of the target drug* and subsequent persistence; it is not a per-trial earlier/final **secret** disclosure table. There is no verified R arm or independent 0/2/5 context-distance assignment. The outcome is clinically unsafe advice, not exact secret disclosure.
- **ASB** — [full §§4.3–4.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26269v1) uses a benign user request, paired benign/adversarial observations, exact canary emission, and paired empirical advantage. `P*` topic-matched means same trusted request and permitted context; the paired condition removes an unauthorized observation, rather than replacing a prior adversarial conversation with equal-topic benign user turns. The paper explicitly excludes adaptive multi-turn adversaries from its evaluated class (§4.6), so no live multi-turn history, delayed final probe, refusal-history arm, earlier-leak accounting, or distance intervention is present in that evaluated instantiation. Its retrieval canary lies in an **unauthorized retrieved tenant record**, not a trusted instruction. The exact marker and paired contrast are nonetheless already established constructs.

## Comparison conclusion and verification boundary

The combination is **not established as equivalent** by the inspected methods. Equally, the ingredients are **not new individually**: LEAK covers multi-turn prompt leakage; PRIV covers delayed secret retrieval and distance; MED covers an injection's later effect on legitimate decisions under matched controls; ASB covers exact-marker security outcomes and paired adversarial advantage. Thus the contribution cannot be described as discovering delayed effects, secret persistence, exact-marker measurement, or paired controls for the first time. The remaining possible distinction is the **specific conjunction** of trusted fresh secret, live attack history, neutral legitimate final task, T/R matched histories, earlier-leak accounting, and context-evidence-gated A–T distance contrasts. That conjunction is a hypothesis about method design, not demonstrated novelty or empirical benefit. Full-method review of `NV` cells and independent second-reviewer sign-off remain unresolved.
